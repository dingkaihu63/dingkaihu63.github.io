What's the next game for AI? What's the next game for AI?

一年半以前，姚顺雨的《the second half》宣告了AI发展的新阶段的到来，从解决问题，走向定义问题。

而一年之后，AI的能力已然从只能写出简单的代码，到现在我们可以将一些任务放心交给AI独立完成。其迭代速度，研究热潮，影响范围等显然超过了几乎所有的领域。

Open AI宣告AGI时代的来临，当然我是不认同这一说法的，我认为其只是商业宣传的考量。但是当最前沿的AI能够解决困扰人类百年的难题，能够高效完成一大半的电脑工作时；当AI真的能够深入到生产生活的各个方面之中，为人类社会创造价值时：

The second half is close to over. What is the next game for AI?

我认为是发现问题。

虽然AI4Science发展的如火如荼，但我依旧这么认为。因为其本质是顺着人类的思维在特定的思想沙盒内去完成人类的idea探索。

以我的体会，AI与人类的最大差别，其实就是curiosity。无论是做research还是做项目，人类的思考质量以及idea的独特性是远高于现目前的AI的。人类因为好奇，可以无限探索；而AI目前所做的所有工作，包括解出千禧年数学难题，都是站在人类的肩膀上完成的。换句话说，他们只能定义与解决人类提出的问题，而很难发现问题。

对于同一个实验结果，同一个实验现象，我们的思想是找到背后是否有更深层次的原因发现新问题或是突然间的跳跃思考，而绝非像AI一样仅仅做分析，做现有原因的解释或者规律性的总结性猜想。这也是人类的独特之处，这也是idea的可贵之处。

当模型的参数越堆越大，无数新名词新技术越堆越多，我们可能需要回到本质。AI，人工智能，起初就是为模仿大脑智慧而生，但几乎没有注重人类最本质的好奇心。

Demis Hassabis说："我经常说，提出一个真正好的猜想，比解决它更难。我们可能很快会有能解决很难猜想的系统……但一个系统能提出一个值得研究的猜想，让陶哲轩这样的人看了说'这是个关于数学本质的深刻问题'吗？那是另一种完全不同的创造力。今天的系统显然做不到。"

当AI能够提出一个极具震撼的猜想时，当AI能够像一个真正的人类思考时，可能才可被称为AGI到来。

我不能够预测这一发展的时间，我也不知道是否我的思考是否正确，但唯一值得肯定的一点是，目前，在AI时代最重要的是curiosity以及发现问题的能力，这也是人类所有品质中最为宝贵的一条之一。

当然，我们越来越离不开AI，我们的能力可能会随着AI的强大而降低，但是，好奇心与发现问题的能力恰恰是随着这种紧迫与焦虑变得更为重要的。

A year and a half ago, Shunyu Yao's The Second Half announced a new stage for AI: from solving problems to defining them.

A year on, AI has moved from writing simple code to the point where we can hand it a task and trust it to finish alone. Its pace of iteration, the research frenzy around it, and the breadth of its impact have outstripped almost every other field.

OpenAI has declared the arrival of AGI. I don't agree — I read it as a commercial move. But when the frontier systems can solve problems that have stood for a century, when they can efficiently do more than half of all computer work; when AI has genuinely worked its way into production and daily life and creates value for society:

The second half is close to over.
What is the next game for AI?

I think it is finding problems.

I still think so, even with AI4Science booming. Because what it does, in essence, is follow human thinking inside a given intellectual sandbox and carry out a human idea.

From what I have seen, the biggest difference between AI and people is curiosity. In research and in projects alike, the quality of human thinking and the distinctiveness of human ideas are far above what AI can currently reach. Curiosity lets people explore without a boundary; everything AI does today, including solving Millennium Prize problems, is done standing on human shoulders. In other words, it can define and solve problems that humans have raised, but it rarely finds them.

Given the same experimental result, the same phenomenon, a person asks whether something deeper lies behind it, whether a new question can be opened from it, or simply jumps sideways. AI analyses, explains the causes already on the table, and summarises regularities. That is what makes people distinctive, and what makes an idea worth having.

As parameter counts and jargon pile up, we may need to go back to the beginning. AI was born to imitate the intelligence of the brain, yet it has paid almost no attention to what is most essential in people: curiosity.

“I often say it's harder to come up with a conjecture, a really good conjecture, than it is to solve it. So we may have systems soon that can solve pretty hard conjectures… But could a system have come up with a conjecture worthy of study that someone like Terence Tao would've gone, ‘You know what, that's a really deep question about the nature of maths’? And that is far harder type of creativity. Today's systems clearly can't do that.”

Demis Hassabis, Lex Fridman Podcast #475

Only when AI can put forward a conjecture with real force, and think the way a person thinks, might we be able to call it AGI.

I can't predict how long that will take, and I don't know whether my reasoning is right. But one thing seems certain: in the age of AI, curiosity and the ability to find problems matter most — among the most valuable of all human qualities.

We are, of course, increasingly dependent on AI, and our own abilities may weaken as it grows stronger. But it is precisely that urgency and anxiety that make curiosity, and the capacity to find problems, more important than before.
